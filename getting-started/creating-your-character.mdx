Your character is the lens through which every story in Ashburn Creek unfolds. A well-considered name, a grounded backstory, and a clear sense of who your character is — and is not — will make your first scenes more engaging and give other players something real to interact with. This page guides you through building a character ready for life on the frontier.

## Choosing a Name

Pick a name that fits the world: the American frontier, circa 1900. Think of names you might find in a period newspaper, on a ranch deed, or carved into a wooden grave marker.

<Note>
  Your character name must be **original**. Do not use the name of a real historical person, a fictional character from any book, film, or game, or anything that pulls another player out of the setting. Your Discord nickname must also reflect your primary character's name.
</Note>

A few practical guidelines when choosing a name:

- **First and last name** — give your character a full name with a believable surname.
- **Period-appropriate spelling** — names like Elias, Colton, Mabel, or Ruth fit the era. Avoid modern invented spellings.
- **Say it aloud** — you and other players will use this name in voice roleplay, so pick something that sounds natural when spoken.

## Writing a Backstory

Your backstory does not need to be long. A focused paragraph or two is enough to ground your character and give you clear direction when you step into the world for the first time.

A useful backstory answers three questions:

1. **Where did your character come from?** A ranch hand drifting west, a sawbones who lost their practice, a widow taking over a homestead — a simple origin gives you context.
2. **What does your character want?** A goal, however modest, drives interaction. Money, safety, justice, a fresh start — something worth working toward.
3. **Why would your character talk to a stranger?** This is the most important question. Characters who have a reason to engage — looking for work, new in town, running from something — create scenes naturally.

<Tip>
  Leave gaps in your backstory on purpose. The best parts of your character's history are the ones that get filled in through play — relationships, failures, and unexpected turns that you could not have scripted in advance.
</Tip>

## Behaviour and Immersion

Ashburn Creek is a story-driven, lore-friendly server. Everything your character says and does should feel like it belongs in the world.

### Stay in Character

Once you are in a scene, stay in character. Save out-of-character communication for appropriate channels — do not drop OOC comments into voice roleplay. If you need to step away, log out rather than going AFK in the world.

### Speak the Era

Your character lives in 1900, not 2024. Use period-appropriate language. Avoid:

- Modern slang or internet phrases
- References to technology, events, or culture from after the setting
- Supernatural or fantastical roleplay that does not belong in a grounded frontier world

You do not need to speak in a perfect historical dialect — natural, measured speech works fine. The goal is simply not to break another player's immersion.

<Warning>
  Fail RP — using modern language, referencing out-of-era concepts, or acting in ways that break the setting — is a rule violation. Staff handles these situations on a case-by-case basis.
</Warning>

### Create Before You Conflict

Roleplay over gunplay is a core principle of the server. Build a storyline first. Legitimate interaction is required before attacking another player. Characters who leap to violence without prior engagement break the story for everyone involved.

## Your First Day: Practical Tips

<Tip>
  Your first session is not the time to make enemies. Start slow, introduce yourself, and focus on building at least one or two relationships before you pursue larger goals.
</Tip>

- **Find the town first.** Get familiar with the layout. Note where the doctor's office, the saloon, and the sheriff's office are — these are the natural hubs of activity.
- **Approach someone in character.** A simple greeting and an in-character reason to talk ("I just rode in from up north, looking for work") opens more doors than standing around waiting.
- **React to the world.** Let what you see and hear shape your character's decisions. The environment and other players' actions are the story — follow them.
- **Ask questions in-character.** Your character can be new in town and not know things. Use that. It invites other players to teach, guide, and involve you in what they are doing.
- **Open a ticket if you are unsure.** When you hit a situation where you are not certain of the rules or the right call, stop and ask staff through a ticket before acting.

## Character Limits and Multiple Characters

If you play more than one character, keep them completely separate.

<Note>
  Items and money **cannot be shared between your characters**. Each character lives independently within the world. Using one character to benefit another — through shared currency, items, or information — is a rule violation.
</Note>

Each character you create is its own story. Treat them that way, and you will find both more rewarding.
