Ashburn Creek uses integrated in-game voice chat — you do not need Discord, TeamSpeak, or any other third-party application to speak with players around you. Your voice transmits directly through the game at a range you control, keeping every conversation tied to where your character is standing and making proximity-based roleplay feel natural. A working microphone is required to participate fully in scenes.

<Tip>
  Test your microphone before logging into an active server session. Discovering a faulty input device mid-scene is disruptive for you and the players around you. A quick check in your system's sound settings takes less than a minute.
</Tip>

## Setting Up Voice Chat

<Steps>
  <Step title="Select your microphone in Windows (or your OS)">
    Open your system sound settings and set your headset or microphone as the **Default Input Device**. If you have multiple audio devices, make sure the right one is active — RedM will use whatever your system reports as default unless you override it manually in the RedM audio settings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the input device inside RedM">
    Launch RedM, navigate to **Settings → Audio**, and verify that the correct input device is selected. If you changed your system default after RedM was already open, restart RedM fully so it picks up the new device.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set an appropriate volume level">
    Speak at your normal roleplay volume and watch the input meter. Aim for a level that is clearly audible to nearby players without clipping or overpowering the scene. Avoid shouting into the mic — adjust gain in your system settings if your voice comes through too hot.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set your in-game voice range">
    Once you are in the server, press `J` to open the voice range selector. Choose the lowest range that suits what your character is doing — a private conversation at a bar table calls for a tighter range than addressing a crowd in the town square. Changing your range mid-scene is normal and expected.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stay in character while speaking">
    Speak as your character whenever your microphone is open. Out-of-character chatter during active scenes breaks immersion for everyone nearby. If you need to communicate out of character, step away from the scene or use the appropriate OOC channel.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Voice Range Reference

Press `J` at any time to cycle through the available voice ranges. As a general guide:

| Range | Best used for |
|-------|--------------|
| Whisper | Private exchanges at very close distance |
| Normal | Standard conversation between two or three people |
| Shout | Calling across a room, addressing a group, or outdoor announcements |

Always start at the lowest range that fits the scene and increase only if needed.

## Troubleshooting Voice Issues

If other players cannot hear you — or you cannot hear them — work through these checks before opening a support ticket.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Verify your input device">
    Go back to your system sound settings and confirm your microphone is still set as the default input. Device selections can reset after a system update or when you plug in new hardware.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Restart RedM after changing devices">
    RedM reads your audio device on launch. If you switched microphones or changed any audio settings while the game was running, a full restart is required for the change to take effect.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check that your mic is not muted">
    Some headsets and audio interfaces have a hardware mute button. Confirm the mute light is off and that your operating system has not muted the device in its privacy or sound settings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open a support ticket if the issue persists">
    If voice still fails after the checks above, open a support ticket in the Ashburn Creek Discord. Include your input device name, a description of what you hear (or don't), and any error messages you have seen.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  General connection and performance issues that affect voice alongside other controls are covered in the [Troubleshooting Common Issues](/support/troubleshooting) guide.
</Note>
