Ashburn Creek's roleplay commands let you communicate your character's actions, emotions, and surroundings directly through the chat channel visible to nearby players. Pairing these commands with the stance system gives every scene depth and visual context without ever breaking immersion. Learn the commands below, practice them before entering a busy scene, and you will be telling compelling frontier stories in no time.

## Roleplay Commands

Type any of the following commands directly into the chat box. Each one formats your message differently so that other players immediately understand whether you are describing an action, adding environmental detail, or flagging a wound.

| Command | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| `/me` | Describes your character's action or physical expression — visible to nearby players as a third-person narrative |
| `/do` | Adds scene details or describes something observable in the environment around your character |
| `/try` | Shows your character attempting an action whose outcome is uncertain — leaves room for other players to respond |
| `/hurt` | Communicates your character's visible wounds or pain to nearby players |
| `/tag` | Adds a visual description of something about your character (clothing detail, a scar, an item they are carrying) |
| `/kys` | Immediately downs your character — intended only for use when you are physically stuck and cannot move |
| `/guide` | Displays helpful in-game tips *(coming soon)* |

<Warning>
  `/kys` is a last-resort unstick tool, not a roleplay death command. Using it outside of a genuine stuck situation — or to circumvent an active roleplay scene — may result in staff action against your account. If you are unsure whether a situation qualifies, contact a staff member first.
</Warning>

## Stance Commands

Stances let your character hold a specific pose or looping animation to reinforce what is happening in a scene — leaning against a post while chatting, kneeling beside an injured rider, or sitting by the fire with a cup of coffee. Enter a stance by typing `/s` followed by the stance name.

```
/s <stancename>
```

For example, to sit at a ground-level campfire, type `/s sitg`. To stand up and clear the stance, type `/s stand`.

<Info>
  Stances support roleplay scenes but never replace them. Keep using `/me`, `/do`, and other RP commands to narrate what your character is actually doing — a stance only shows the pose, not the story behind it.
</Info>
