Out on the frontier, a good doctor is worth more than gold. Gunshot wounds, riding accidents, brawls, and the unforgiving wilderness send a steady stream of injured souls through the surgery door, and it falls to you to keep them breathing. Playing a doctor or field medic in Ashburn Creek is one of the most story-rich roles available — you are present at some of the most dramatic moments in other characters' lives, and the care you provide has real consequences for the community around you.

## Whitelisted Status & Queue Priority

The doctor role is a **whitelisted position**. You must receive staff approval through a support ticket before you begin practising medicine in an official capacity. Once approved, your account is granted **queue priority**, moving you ahead in the connection queue during busy periods. See [Queue Priority](/getting-started/queue-priority) for a full explanation of how that system works.

---

## Responsibilities

A doctor in Ashburn Creek is more than someone who hands out bandages. You are a community pillar — someone other players seek out in their most vulnerable moments.

**Core responsibilities:**

- Respond to injury calls and treat wounded players with full roleplay — describe what you observe, what you are doing, and what the patient should expect
- Run structured medical scenes that give injured players an active, meaningful experience rather than a passive wait
- Provide care during and after conflicts, staying neutral where possible and prioritising patient welfare over faction loyalty
- Maintain a realistic, consistent medical persona — your character should have a believable reason to be practising medicine in a frontier town
- Keep records of notable cases where relevant, supporting ongoing story threads that involve injury or illness

---

## Medical Roleplay Standards

The server rules require that **all players stay honest and realistic during medical scenes**. As a doctor, you set the tone for those scenes, and the standard is higher for you than for anyone else.

Describe injuries in terms that make sense for the era and the circumstances. A man shot with a revolver at close range is not going to walk out of your surgery in five minutes. A broken leg sustained in a riding accident will take time and rest to heal. Play the medicine, not the convenience.

<Tip>
  **Keep your medicine era-appropriate.** In 1900, frontier doctors worked with carbolic acid, laudanum, chloroform, and basic surgical instruments — not sterile modern equipment. Reference the tools and treatments of the time: suturing wounds with silk thread, setting bones by hand, using whiskey as an antiseptic in a pinch, administering laudanum for pain. Small period-accurate details make medical scenes far more immersive for everyone involved. When in doubt, err toward grit and realism over speed and convenience.
</Tip>

Always ask for consent before running a prolonged or invasive medical scene — particularly if the scene involves serious injury, amputation, or death. Give your patient a clear sense of what to expect before you begin, and check in during the scene if the tone shifts significantly.

---

## Tonics, Revive Stims, and Active PvP

Medical items exist in the world for a reason, but their use during conflict is restricted. **Players may not use tonics or revive stims during active PvP to re-enter a fight.** This rule protects the integrity of combat roleplay and ensures that deaths and injuries carry genuine weight.

As a doctor, you may be called to a scene where conflict is still ongoing. Exercise caution and good judgement. Treating a downed player mid-gunfight may be dramatic and heroic — but it should feel earned and story-appropriate, not a mechanical exploit to keep someone in the fight.

---

## Doctor Corruption

A frontier doctor is not always above reproach. Corrupt or morally compromised medical roleplay — patching up known outlaws, providing false testimony about a patient's condition, deliberately withholding care — is a legitimate story avenue in Ashburn Creek, but it carries the same restrictions as any other form of corruption.

<Warning>
  **Any corruption storyline involving the doctor role must be approved by staff through a support ticket before you begin.** Outline your intentions clearly and wait for explicit approval. Unapproved corrupt medical roleplay will be treated as a rule violation.
</Warning>

## How to Apply

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the Rules">
    Review the [Roleplay Rules](/rules/roleplay-rules) and [Community Standards](/rules/community-standards) before submitting an application. Medical roleplay touches some of the most sensitive moments in other characters' stories — knowing the rules thoroughly is essential.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build Your Character">
    Decide who your doctor is and why they are in Ashburn Creek. Did they study back East and head West for a fresh start? Are they a self-taught field surgeon with battlefield experience? A clear backstory makes your application stronger and your roleplay richer. See [Creating Your Character](/getting-started/creating-your-character) for guidance.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit a Support Ticket">
    Open a support ticket in the Discord server. Introduce your character, explain the kind of medical roleplay you intend to run, and describe how you see the doctor role serving the community. Staff will review your application and may ask follow-up questions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Await Staff Approval">
    Do not begin practising medicine in an official capacity until staff confirms your approval in writing. Once approved, you will receive queue priority and any relevant in-game access needed to fulfil the role.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the Surgery">
    Set up your practice, introduce yourself to the community, and start making yourself known. Players will find you — especially after the first big gunfight rolls through town.
  </Step>
</Steps>
