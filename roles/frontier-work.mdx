Not every story in Ashburn Creek begins with a badge or a doctor's bag. The town runs on the labour of ranchers, traders, craftspeople, and the wandering souls who pass through with something to sell and nowhere particular to be. These independent livelihoods require no staff approval and no whitelist application — you can pick up a trade on your first day in the world and start building your character's story from the ground up.

## Open to Every Player

Frontier work roles are available to all players from the moment they arrive. There is no application process, no waiting period, and no formal approval required. You begin by deciding who your character is and what they do to survive, and then you live it out in the world through roleplay and interaction.

That freedom comes with a responsibility: **every livelihood should generate scenes with other players.** A rancher who keeps entirely to themselves, a trader who never haggles, a gambler who sits alone — these are missed opportunities. The richest stories on Ashburn Creek grow from contact between characters. Use your trade as a reason to meet people, create conflict, offer services, and leave an impression.

## Ranching & Land Work

Ranching is hard, honest work. You tend horses, manage livestock, maintain land, and keep the kind of operation that feeds and supplies a frontier town. As a rancher, you interact with traders who want your cattle, lawmen who investigate rustling, and neighbours who may not always respect your fences.

**Ways to engage as a rancher:**

- Run a working ranch with horses, cattle, or both — give it a name and make it feel like a real place
- Hire hands from among other players and create an employment dynamic
- Deal with rustlers, disputes over land, and the seasonal hardships of frontier farming
- Supply goods to merchants and create ongoing trade relationships

---

## Commerce & Trade

The town needs merchants. Bartenders, general store keepers, travelling traders, and supply haulers fill a role that makes Ashburn Creek feel like a living place rather than a backdrop. Commerce characters are naturally social — your trade puts you in contact with almost every other player in the world.

**Ways to engage in commerce:**

- Set up a fixed location — a saloon, a general store, a trading post — and tend it regularly
- Travel between locations with goods to sell, making yourself a known face on the road
- Establish credit, debt, and ongoing trade agreements that create story threads over time
- Use your position as a merchant to gather information, play factions against each other, or stay carefully neutral

## Craftwork & Services

The frontier runs on skilled hands. Blacksmiths keep horses shod and tools sharp. Carpenters build the structures everyone else takes for granted. Tailors keep townsfolk presentable. Cooks feed the hungry at the end of a long ride. Barbers are the first person you talk to when you need to feel human again.

Craftwork roles are deeply immersive because they create natural, low-stakes interaction. You do not need a crisis to visit the blacksmith — you just need a loose horseshoe. That ordinary contact is where a lot of the best roleplay happens.

**Frontier crafts and services to consider:**

- Blacksmithing — weapons maintenance, horseshoes, tools, ironwork
- Carpentry — building repairs, furniture, wagon work
- Tailoring — clothing, alterations, period-appropriate fashion
- Cooking — trail food, saloon meals, provisions for long rides
- Barbering — shaves, haircuts, and conversation

## Independent / Drifter

Not everyone who rides into Ashburn Creek has a plan. Hunters and trappers work the wilderness for pelts and game. Gamblers drift from table to table looking for the next score. Drifters take odd jobs, follow rumours, and end up in the middle of other people's stories through sheer proximity.

The independent path is the most flexible livelihood in the server. You are not tied to a location, a schedule, or a set of obligations. Your character can evolve freely in response to whatever the world throws at them.

<Tip>
  **Create scenes deliberately, even when playing solo.** Riding alone into town and sitting at the bar is a starting point, not an arrival. Introduce yourself to the bartender. Ask the stranger next to you where they have been. Show off the pelts you just brought in. The frontier rewards characters who reach out — other players are looking for exactly the same connection you are. One conversation can spark a storyline that runs for weeks. Start small and follow the thread wherever it leads.
</Tip>

## Starting Tips

If you are new to Ashburn Creek and unsure where to begin, start with something simple and concrete. Pick one thing your character does to survive — hauling goods, tending horses, hunting game — and do it visibly, in places where other players can find you. Let the work create opportunities for conversation, and let those conversations point you toward your next story beat.

You do not need a dramatic backstory or a grand plan. You need a reason to be in town and something to do with your hands. Everything else follows from there.
